# The World Took Its Next Step

*One region dissolves. Adjacent pairs persist. The GPU proposes again from the state it became.*

Last time, the world had a beginning. Now it has a continuation.

The geometric world running on my RTX 3070 has taken its third committed step. It removed all twelve disagreements around its seeded region, recorded the result, and proposed again from the state it had just become. No reloaded starting field. No new move supplied for that second proposal.

Then the pair experiments delivered the contrast: under the same transition law, every adjacent pair tested stayed bound.

One region dissolves. Adjacent pairs persist. And the world’s own result becomes the input to its next computation.

The new paper, [*Genesis on the GPU: A World That Proposes Its Own Next Step*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23673872), is now published with its supporting records under Apache 2.0.

## Twelve walls come down

In [the October 22 post](https://metacortexdynamics.substack.com/p/genesis-on-the-gpu), one captured hardware event had given the world its beginning. Twelve of its 480 flags read THIS. The other 468 read NO. Across 720 overlaps, the world recorded where neighbouring readings agreed and where they did not. Twelve disagreements formed the seeded region’s boundary. Those are the walls.

The second committed step preserved that field. The next question was what the world’s transition law would actually admit from it.

The rule is strict discharge: remove at least one existing wall and open none. Every offered assignment gets a receipt naming exactly what it removes and what it opens. There is no score to maximize and no preference to break a draw.

The GPU evaluated all 1,924 assignments in the refined incidence family. Exactly one satisfied the rule: the whole twelve-flag region reads NO.

Twelve walls discharged. None opened. None retained.

That was the third committed step. The seeded region dissolved; the carrier remained. Every flag now read NO and all 720 overlaps agreed.

The result is forced within that family BECAUSE every assignment has a recorded receipt and only one meets strict discharge. The uniqueness is in the completed computation.

## The next input is what the world became

The successor did not sit at the end of a report. Execution returned it directly to the same proposal operation.

That operation enumerated 1,924 offers again. The assignments had the same geometric supports, but their receipts changed BECAUSE they were now being evaluated on the returned uniform field.

This time, no offer was strict. There were no walls left to discharge.

That distinction matters: settlement did not prevent the next proposal. The world had changed, and its own changed state supplied the next computation.

We tested the dependency. Reloading the old field brought back the old receipts and its single strict exit; it did not reproduce the renewed proposal. Missing-return controls withheld the continuation. Replays reproduced the corresponding readbacks byte for byte, and reversing enumeration preserved the decoded result.

Three committed steps, followed by a witnessed proposal on the returned state. That is the loop the new paper reports.

## Two neighbouring regions stay bound

Now put two adjacent site regions into a separate diagnostic field, both reading THIS, with everything else reading NO. Apply the same strict-discharge test.

Every one of the refined carrier’s 120 adjacent pairs had no strict exit.

We then added the parent-level assignments carried by the refinement ancestry. That expanded the test to 2,024 distinct assignments per refined field. Every pair still held.

The same experiment on the parent carrier covered all thirty adjacent pairs, each against its own 484 assignments. Again, no strict exit. A single-site control had exactly one strict exit; a uniform control had nothing to discharge.

These pairs are separate experimental fields, not structures born during the three-step history. Their persistence is the result of exhausting the stated assignments under the same law used for the seeded region.

There is no extra instruction saying “keep pairs alive.” Their boundaries remain BECAUSE none of the offered assignments removes a wall without opening another.

That is what makes this result exciting. Change and persistence come out of the same construction. The configurations differ; the test does not.

## An ending and a starting point

The world in the October 22 post now has an ending to its strict-discharge sequence: uniform agreement. The pair diagnostics have a different ending: a non-uniform boundary that holds.

The paper puts those results beside the renewed-proposal witness, the replay checks and the controls. Its evidence records are public. You can follow the claim from the operation to the receipt instead of taking a rendered picture as the result.

Next come triples, rings and larger configurations. Can a strict transition lead into a new bound structure instead of dissolving the region? Which arrangements persist together? Those questions now have a construction on which to run.

Four days ago, the story was a world that could record its own beginning. Today it has carried its own successor into its next proposal—and the same transition law has produced both dissolution and persistent structure.
